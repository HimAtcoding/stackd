# Metrics

## North star

> **My note:** The braindump lists 12 metrics and no primary one, which in practice means the flattering number becomes the primary one. Pick this instead:
>
> **Students who return in a later week and add something to their plan.**
>
> It captures the only thing that matters at this stage: the tool was useful enough to come back to, and useful enough to act in. Everything below is diagnostic for that one number.

## Worth tracking

- Weekly and monthly active students
- Return rate by week
- Requirements searched
- Course results viewed per session
- Courses saved to a plan
- Colleges represented among users
- Universities and majors targeted
- User-reported data errors
- Sessions ending with zero results (the clearest signal of a coverage gap)

## Worth ignoring

Total registered users, page views, and time on site. All three go up when the product is confusing.

## Qualitative, and more valuable than any of the above at this stage

Ten students, watched using it without explanation. Record confusion, errors, missing features, wrong data, and the questions they ask out loud. Then fix the biggest problem and do it again with fifty.

## Honesty rule

Do not manufacture or exaggerate impact metrics. "Estimated planning time saved" is a modeled number, not a measurement — if it's ever used in a pitch or on a flyer, the methodology has to be stated next to it.
